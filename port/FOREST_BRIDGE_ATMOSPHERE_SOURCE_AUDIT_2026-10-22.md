# Forest bridge lower-atmosphere source audit — 2026-10-22

## Verdict

The darker, crisper lower bridge/ground in the current-head 1-second still relative to saved Steam screenshot 14 is a real **unmatched-image observation**, not an isolated port defect. The strongest source-backed contributor is the forest's authored fog sprites 119/122 being only partway through their six-second fade near level entry, while the Steam image has unknown level-relative time. The dark lower shrub band is authored parallax ID37, not missing fog. No art, XML, shader, or rendering change is justified by this pair.

## Evidence and discriminators

- Current evidence is `port/build/current-head-visual-capture-2026-10-22/still-1s.png` (1280×720, extracted from the identified 10-second active-gameplay MP4 at HEAD `0cdce25f`; capture provenance in `CURRENT_HEAD_VISUAL_CAPTURE_2026-10-22.md`). Reference is `../store_media/screenshots/14.jpg` (1920×1080 JPEG). Both show the forest bridge/raft neighborhood, but player, branch/pickup and foreground registration are not exact. Linux HUD shows 2 orbs/near-full health; Steam shows 13/reduced health. Recording 1 s is not itself a measured *level-relative simulation* time. Steam has no camera, frame, weather or postprocess phase sidecar.
- The Steam JPEG contains black top/bottom bands: visible content is approximately source rows 50–1029 (`FOREST_MATCHED_VISUAL_NEXT_2026-10-22.md`). Uniform 2/3 scaling with bands masked is the appropriate first registration; vertically stretching the cropped content would manufacture about 10% Y distortion. JPEG/store processing can affect apparent edge softness. Neither image pair nor JPEG proves a blur/shader deficiency.
- XML `Game/XmlFiles/levels/forest_exploration_1.xml` IDs 119/122 (`:1625–1665`) authors `Media/fog.png`, `kGroundFront`, alphas 0.5/0.6, 6 s fade-in, 0.07 pixel-wobble intensity and 0.3 speed modifier; ID122 is vertically flipped. Original Windows `Game/src/GameObjects/ScrollingSprite.cpp:22–55` computes opacity as original alpha × elapsed/6 before full opacity and scrolls both sprites. Linux source has the corresponding fade/update (`port/game/LevelScene.cpp`, documented in `FOREST_FOG_FADE_TIMING_AUDIT_2026-10-22.md`). Windows `BasicLightTexture_PixelWobble.fx` perturbs UVs with time/sine then multiplies sampled alpha by that opacity: this is a moving translucent texture, not a screen-space blur. Thus less haze can expose crisper underlying bridge/grass edges without any sharpness bug.
- Prior **controlled same-frame GPU** hide-ID evidence (`FOREST_HAZE_GPU_DISCRIMINATOR_2026-10-22.md`) proves IDs 119/122 are actually rendered: at frame 120, hiding both changes 274,460 pixels and lowers ground ROI mean RGB from `(50.13,43.19,30.74)` to `(37.72,32.56,23.25)`; by frame 360, the fog-contribution mean absolute RGB in that ROI is more than twice frame 120's. Different geometry at the two times bars treating this as a literal opacity ratio, but it discriminates against “missing fog.” The separate float-alpha correction changes the frame-300 fog region by at most one RGB code value (`FOG_FLOAT_ALPHA_CONTROLLED_COMPARISON_2026-10-22.md`), too small to explain the broad visual difference by itself.
- XML ID37 (`:612–635`) is a 0.95-alpha, camera-following `kMiddleForeground` `shrubbery.png` parallax layer. Its original V orientation and camera-X UV scroll were previously corrected in the port (`ID37_FIDELITY_DIAGNOSTIC.md`). A controlled hide-ID37 frame changes only y≥597 in the earlier bridge-area capture; hiding fog reaches up to y≈246 at frame 120. These distinct spatial footprints separate the authored dark lower matte from the broader pale veil. Original `Sepia.fx` mixes channels after the scene; it is a global color transform, not evidence of a missing local ground-blur pass.
