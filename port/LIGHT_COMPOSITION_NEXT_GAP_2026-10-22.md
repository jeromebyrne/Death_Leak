# Next forest-1 light-composition gap — damage burst tint (2026-10-22)

## Verdict

The most consequential *distinct, source-proven* omission found in this lighting/effect audit is the short, bright **per-character damage burst**. In Windows, a damageable `SolidMovingSprite` struck during gameplay has its `effectlighttexture` RGB multiplied by a time-decaying scalar, initially near 5 and returning to 1 over 0.25 seconds. The Linux player and enemy sprite paths have no corresponding per-hit light-color state/modulation. This affects the focal character and damageable enemies during combat, but only on damage frames; it is not a scene-wide ambient light or an explanation for the persistent ID37 dark band/ID15 pale shaft. No matched-frame pixel delta or overall parity is claimed.

## Source-to-port evidence

- Forest-1 Player ID2 explicitly selects `effectlighttexture` (`Game/XmlFiles/levels/forest_exploration_1.xml:33-40`). Windows effect binding maps that name to `EFFECT_LIGHT_TEXTURE` (`Game/src/GameObjects/DrawableObject.cpp:133-145`), and `Sprite::Draw` dispatches it to `Draw_effectLightTexture` (`Game/src/GameObjects/Sprite.cpp:369-380`). `EffectLightTexture` loads `BasicLightTexture.fx` and initializes `lightColor=(1,1,1,1)` (`Game/src/Effects/EffectLightTexture.cpp:4-7,29-48`). Its pixel shader computes `lightColor * sampled RGBA`, then multiplies alpha by sprite alpha (`Game/ShaderFiles/BasicLightTexture.fx:53-59`). This is **texture-local RGB gain before blending**, not a light map or full-screen pass.
- The actual trigger is `SolidMovingSprite::OnDamage`: when damageable it resets `mBurstTintStartTime` (`Game/src/GameObjects/SolidMovingSprite.cpp:464-477`). The player calls through `Character::OnDamage` to that base method (`Game/src/GameObjects/Player.cpp:181-183`; `Game/src/GameObjects/Character.cpp:1768-1770`). At draw time `Sprite::Draw_effectLightTexture` computes `timeDiff = totalTime - start`, sets RGB `val = 1 + (4 - 4*timeDiff/0.25)` while `timeDiff < 0.25`, applies the pass, then restores shared effect `lightColor` to white (`Game/src/GameObjects/Sprite.cpp:17,626-672`). The reset after the pass matters: later sprites do not inherit the flash. `mShowingBurstTint` is set/cleared but is **not** tested in the draw branch; timing, not that boolean, gates the visible gain. Do not implement a persistent tint based on the flag alone.
- The port has live damage paths: `Combat::DamageEnemy` subtracts enemy health (`port/game/Combat.cpp:146-156`), enemy projectile hits subtract player health (`:422-433`), and solid-contact damage is applied in `port/linux/main.cpp:697-698` through `Combat::ApplySolidContactDamage` (`port/game/Combat.h:68`). Yet `Combat::AppendPainterItems` builds enemy `render::Sprite`s with default white tint and no hit-time color (`port/game/Combat.cpp:490-505`); `LevelScene::Draw` copies the player object's static tint into its body/arm draws (`port/game/LevelScene.cpp:892-915,933-941`). Generic `SDLRenderer::DrawSprite` supports byte RGB modulation via `SDL_SetTextureColorMod` (`port/linux/SDLRenderer.cpp:173-190`) but receives no damage burst. Since the source gain exceeds 1, merely setting an SDL byte tint cannot reproduce it: white 255 is already the modulation ceiling.
- Windows orders drawable objects by depth and draws each effect with its own texture and alpha (`Game/src/GameObjects/GameObjectManager.cpp:302-330`); this burst is applied at that sprite's draw, before the existing scene blend/postprocess. The port's scene and combat painter items likewise give a bounded insertion point, without changing global sepia or authored XML/PNG.

## Bounded implementation recommendation

Add a 0.25-second hit-age/timestamp only to damageable port actors (player and `Combat::Enemy`), reset on the corresponding *accepted* damage event. At their existing painter slots, draw their normal texture/animation/alpha with per-sprite pre-blend RGB gain `5 - 16*age` for `0 <= age < 0.25`, otherwise 1; keep alpha unchanged. A shader or isolated additive/gain path is needed for values above 1, with saturated output matching the source render target; do not approximate by bleaching the entire screen or applying alpha gain. Preserve the source's post-draw white reset semantics across subsequent sprites. Start with player/enemy hit flashes, and separately inspect any other `SolidMovingSprite` actually represented by the port before expanding coverage. Verify a deterministic hit at age 0, 0.125, and >=0.25 using isolated actor crops and a no-hit neighbor; Windows matched-frame validation remains necessary for pixel parity.

## Limits and prior-work boundary

This is a read-only source audit; no build, GPU/ALSA run, capture, shared source/status edit, or claim of measured visual impact. Earlier `FOREST_EFFECT_GAP` covers float fog opacity, while the `NEXT_VISUAL_*` reports cover particle brightness alpha, player shadow/aim line, projectile fade, and weather flash; this report does not repeat those findings. The ordinary `BasicLightTexture` path is otherwise a white texture sample when no damage burst is active. No forest-1 XML sprite selects `effectlighttexturebump`, so its normal-map shader is not an active forest-1 candidate despite being bound in the Windows engine.
