# Post-fade Forest ID37 occlusion discriminator — private physical-GPU gate

## Decision

**No source-backed ID37 correction is justified.** On the exact binary that made the corrected 13-second fade/gameplay video, hiding authored Forest-1 ID37 at a held late-forest, partly occluded player pose strongly changes the ragged lower foreground but does not restore the player silhouette. The player-region A/B changes are small and confounded by an independently changing full-screen film frame; this capture cannot claim a zero-pixel player effect or matched-original-Windows parity. The earlier film-phase-matched current-clip ID37 gate established zero changes in this player box on a different binary, and its ID10 hide (not ID37 hide) changed the overlapping trunk/player region. This same-binary gate is consistent with that attribution, without transplanting its exact per-pixel result.

The player box overlaps y596–620, yet has no large ID37-like change. The authored dark lower foreground remains an intentional foreground effect, not evidence that ID37 causes the player's late-pose invisibility.

## Next visual gate and lane release

Obtain a **registered, lossless original-Windows Forest-1 frame at this held camera/player pose**, with draw-state for both ID10 and ID37 (object/texture identity, depth/order, camera/UV, alpha, blend, effect and mip/LOD), plus the corresponding Linux frame at a recorded identical film index/postprocess boundary. Compare player silhouette and lower-band interior/edge separately. That is the specific gate that could distinguish intended authored occlusion from a renderer mismatch and justify a narrow correction. Until then, do not remove/brighten ID37 or claim exact Windows fidelity.
