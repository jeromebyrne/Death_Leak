# Private read-only performance follow-up — film/sepia pass fusion

**Candidate, not accepted:** fuse the authored 11-FPS, post-sepia/pre-HUD film texture into the existing Sepia.fx fullscreen fragment pass on ordinary gameplay frames. This is distinct from the completed scene render-target change, text batching, sampler cache, and the rejected private-GL sprite quad. It retains the film image, frame clock, alpha, screen placement, and HUD painter order; it eliminates one separate 1280x720 blended SDL film draw/flush and its framebuffer read/modify/write. Do not implement until the visual-composition owner releases this seam.

## Evidence and bounded expectation

- `PERFORMANCE_CHECKPOINT_STATUS.md`: staged `f3f4a7a` NVE7 Forest-1 B:360 run at 1280x720, FPS on, 120 warmup/240 samples: p50 **20.1383 ms**, p95 **22.0002 ms**. This is ~49.7 FPS median and still 3.47 ms above the 16.667-ms 60-FPS budget.
- `port/linux/main.cpp` around lines 1660–1700: sepia is applied first, then one of four 576x324 authored film PNGs is stretched to 1280x720 at tint alpha 51/255 (64/255 in sea_2), then bars and HUD are drawn. `port/linux/SDLRenderer.cpp` around lines 818–872: sepia already samples the scene target in a fullscreen GL pass. The same renderer's ordinary film sprite path subsequently calls `SDL_RenderCopyF` with blend mode `SDL_BLENDMODE_BLEND`.
- Existing *visual-destructive diagnostic only* (`port/build/performance-next/no-film.log`) measured p50/p95 19.1587/21.1087 ms versus pre-batch diagnostic 20.9694/22.8091 ms. This suggests the film path matters, but is **not** a measured saving for fusion: these are not post-batch matched A/B runs; omitting film is visually invalid and changes GPU work. A plausible ceiling is roughly 1–2 ms/frame, with actual gain likely lower because fused shader still samples and blends film. Even an implausible full 2-ms saving leaves p50 ~18.14 ms, short of 60 FPS.
