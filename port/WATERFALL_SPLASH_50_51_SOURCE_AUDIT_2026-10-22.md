## Finding / bounded handoff

**The next justified slice after ID49 is the paired, small waterfall-top `ParticleSpray` IDs 50 and 51.** They are two near-identical, authored, looping `waterfall_splash.png` emitters, 10 particles each, at the two sides of the upper falls and `kMiddleForeground` depth. ## Authored contract and non-obvious source behavior

- `Game/XmlFiles/levels/waterfall_top.xml:857-898`: ID50 origin `(92.985352,2709)`, ID51 `(572.985352,2697)`; both `kMiddleForeground`, 300×300 object bounds, updateable/not always-update, alpha 1, `effectlighttexture`, occlusion fade off, 10 particles, `Media\\waterfall_splash.png`, spawn spread X=11/Y=4, spread=1, speed **min=5/max=-32**, lifetime .5–1.5 s, size 45–90 with `scaleto=true`/`scaletovalue=4`, gravity 22, loop true/time 0, brightness 1, fade-in/out .25. ID50 direction is `(0,-1)`; ID51 is `(0.01,-1)`. Their 300×300 dimensions are whole-emitter update/draw culling bounds, never a giant textured quad; their particles can be outside those bounds because X/Y spawn offsets reach ±110/±40 source-world units and size grows.
- `Game/src/GameObjects/GameObjectManager.cpp:833-923` reads the XML and calls `CreateDirectedSprayLoadTime`, then requests deferred `Warm(true)`. In `Game/src/Particles/ParticleEmitterManager.cpp:598-705`, `maxSpeed <= minSpeed` deliberately selects **maxSpeed=-32** for every particle; do not sort the range, clamp it to +5, or randomize speeds. Source multiplies speed and initial size by `Game::GetGameScale().X`, and passes `scaleTo * gameScale` to `ParticleSpray`; `Game/src/Game.cpp:142-143` derives game scale from backbuffer/1920×1080. Thus numerical size and growth require the actual Windows backbuffer size before a metric parity claim. At 1920×1080 the target size multiplier is 4; at 1280×720 source scale-to is 8/3, not 4. Port currently takes XML values without this source-scale application (`port/game/LevelScene.cpp:318-324`).
- Per-index lifetime is `1.5 - (1.0/10)*i` (1.5 through .6 seconds), not independently random. For each particle, source consumes a `rand()` call for `spread` but discards its computed value, leaving direction unchanged. It independently picks signed X/Y offsets from integer modulo `spread*10+1` (`ParticleEmitterManager.cpp:604-648`). Brightness remains 1. Because the port has a private LCG and Windows uses global `rand()` consumed by earlier objects, neither matching placements nor exact particle timelines may be asserted without RNG/load-history control.
- `Game/src/Particles/ParticleSpray.cpp:160-342`: draw vertices use **pre-advance** position/size; positive gravity first decays speed by .99 per update, then position advances by `direction * speed * (delta/targetDelta)` and Y subtracts `gravity * (delta/targetDelta)`. With speed -32 and Y direction -1, the speed term moves toward positive world Y before gravity subtracts 22 per nominal tick; ID51 additionally has a small negative X velocity (`.01 * -32` before scaling/decay). This is not ID49's zero-gravity, constant-speed behavior. Size scales after age advances as `startSize * (1 + (scaleTo-1)*age/lifetime)`; expiry resets speed, size, position and age for looping particles. Fade-out's source condition starts when `percentTimeLeft < .75` and divides by .75; fade-in covers the initial .25. `Warm(true)` defers random age/position until after the first ordinary update, and warm Y uses **plus** gravity (`:344-363`), unlike ordinary Update. First vertices can still show pre-warm positions. The port `ParticleSpray.h:43-78` already models these mechanics, including negative speed, .99 drag, scaling, reset, and the warm gravity sign, subject to game-scale/timing/RNG limits.
- Original shader (`Game/ShaderFiles/Particle.fx:52-59`) multiplies sampled RGBA by brightness then alpha by particle fade. Source draws six vertices per particle (`ParticleSpray.cpp:128-157`) when the whole emitter is visible and alpha positive (`GameObjectManager.cpp:302-330`), and updates non-always-update objects only in the camera update rectangle (`:221-257`; `Game/src/Utils/Utilities.h:45-72`). `kMiddleForeground=1000` follows ID49's `kGroundFront=1750` and `kFarForeground=1500` in the depth-ordered painter, but precedes `kNearForeground=500`; retain XML order between ID50 and ID51 and their neighboring same-depth objects (`Game/src/GameObjects/GameObject.h:45-53`, `port/game/LevelScene.cpp:31-41,861-879`).

## Port seam and acceptance boundary

`port/game/LevelScene.cpp:271-328` already parses the relevant spray fields, but its forest-ID47-only guard means 50/51 never load; `spray.xmlId` is also hard-coded to `"47"`. After ID49's separate admission is stable, extend the *specific* predicate to waterfall-top IDs 50 and 51, carry each actual XML ID into filtering, retain distinct XML order/depth and existing per-particle asset. Preserve forest ID47 and ID49 behavior. `LevelScene::UpdateVisuals` (`:665-670`) already uses emitter bounds for approximate zone gating; `drawSpraysBefore` (`:861-879`) merges admitted sprays into depth order. Do not turn bounds into a quad. The port's `ParticleSpray::Draw` (`:79-99`) clips each quad at the viewport but lacks the original whole-emitter camera test; inspect transition cases if an emitter/particle appears at the view edge. Source's culling includes current depth parallax offsets, while the port's zone is direct AABB overlap; that approximation should be documented, not silently called exact.

Minimal verification for the implementing owner: (1) static loader assertion that waterfall-top admits exactly 49/50/51 after the staged changes, with counts 100/10/10, distinct depth 1750/1000/1000, ID-specific direction, negative -32 speed and scale flags; forest still admits only ID47; (2) deterministic port-state checks at known `dt` for ID50/51 first-update/pre-warm draw coordinates, drag/gravity, ID51 X drift, size growth and loop reset, using independently calculated expectations; (3) splash-only and unfiltered painter frames at fixed camera/timeline showing upper-left/upper-right placement and depth relative to neighboring foreground. A Linux-only frame can verify presence/placement, not Windows parity. A matched Windows comparison needs backbuffer/game scale, camera, timeline, RNG history and output size controlled; no such comparison was made here.

Priority is bounded and source-backed: two authored foreground accents absent from the port, with a known negative-speed branch and life-scaling that ID49 does not exercise. Their actual screen coverage or perceptual rank relative to other gaps has not been measured. No speculative parity claim follows from the source audit.
