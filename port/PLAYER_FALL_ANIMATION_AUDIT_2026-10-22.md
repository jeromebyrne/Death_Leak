# Player falling-frame timing source audit — 2026-10-22

Scope: bounded, read-only Windows-vs-Linux source audit of the player falling-body transition identified in `FOREST_ANIMATION_PARITY_AUDIT_2026-10-22.md`. No GPU, display, build, gameplay capture, or code modification. This is not a visual parity claim.

## Source behavior

The forest's authored Player ID 2 uses `XmlFiles/animation/player_anim_default.xml` (`Game/XmlFiles/levels/forest_exploration_1.xml:33-46`). Its body has distinct `JumpingUp` (60 fps) and `JumpingDown` (30 fps) sequences (`Game/XmlFiles/animation/player_anim_default.xml:524,603`). Coordinates are y-up in the port (`port/game/PlayerMotion.h:4-5`), so positive vertical velocity is ascent and negative is descent.

In Windows, `Character::Update` marks upward motion with `m_velocity.Y > -0.5f` when dry, and downward motion in the subsequent branch; it stamps `mMidAirMovingDownStartTime` only on the *transition* into downward motion (`Game/src/GameObjects/Character.cpp:285-316`). These flags also require vertical acceleration (or water), no solid support, and no side collision. `Character::UpdateAnimations` prioritizes impact/melee/roll/crouch before its downward branch (`:735-922`). During that branch it changes to and animates `JumpingDown` only when `totalTime > descentStart + 0.05f`, **or** `GetTimeNotOnSolidSurface() > 0.25f`, **or** water (`:922-959`). Until then, it assigns the current body frame as texture without changing sequence or calling `Animate`; both frame and fractional animation clock remain held. The 0.05 comparison is strict; at exactly 0.05 s the delay has not elapsed. `mTimeNotOnSolidSurface` increments while off a solid and resets on contact (`:394-400`), so a long ascent can make the 0.25-s bypass true at the first downward update. The delay is therefore conditional, not universal for ordinary jumps.

In Linux, `main.cpp:1073-1076` passes `PlayerMotion` velocity and grounded/wall/water/dash state to `LevelScene::UpdatePlayerVisual`. That routine chooses `JumpingUp` for airborne `vy > 0`, otherwise `JumpingDown` immediately (subject to higher-priority melee, roll, water, dash and wall-slide cases), then switches sequence, resets the frame and frame clock, and may advance within the same update (`port/game/LevelScene.cpp:740-791`). It has no descent-transition timer, no held-frame branch, and no elapsed-off-solid input. `PlayerMotion` tracks `airTime_` privately, reset on contact/jump and incremented each airborne update (`port/game/PlayerMotion.cpp:16,73,164-165`), but does not expose it (`port/game/PlayerMotion.h:10-33`).

## Divergence and boundary

For a dry, non-colliding, non-melee/non-roll airborne transition with off-solid time at most 0.25 s, Linux selects the first `JumpingDown` frame as soon as its post-motion velocity becomes nonpositive. Windows holds the previous body frame and clock until the strict 0.05-s descent timer expires (unless its off-solid timer first exceeds 0.25 s). Thus the earlier audit's “up to about 50 ms” is a conditional upper bound, especially relevant to a short hop or drop; it should not be read as applying to every full jump apex. The different Windows dry-state threshold (`-0.5` original per-tick velocity) versus Linux's zero threshold, and their different motion/update ordering, can shift the start tick independently of the delay; this audit does not establish exact frame-for-frame alignment.

## Disposition

A faithful fix needs `LevelScene::UpdatePlayerVisual` and its state in `LevelScene.h`, plus an off-solid elapsed input or equivalent timer from `PlayerMotion`/the caller. The narrow implementation would stamp the first eligible falling update; hold the existing body frame *and its fractional clock* while descent elapsed is at most 0.05 s and off-solid elapsed at most 0.25 s; then select/advance `JumpingDown`, resetting that descent stamp on ascent or landing. It must preserve existing higher-priority states (melee, roll, water, dash, wall slide) and explicitly decide whether to reproduce the source's `-0.5` per-tick threshold. Focused native cases should cover a short apex held through the strict threshold, a >0.25-s-airtime immediate transition, landing/re-ascent reset, and no regression to water/wall/dash precedence.

No fix or native test was added: the necessary `LevelScene.cpp` change intersects shared animation work and the task explicitly prefers a read-only report in that case. Existing unrelated worktree changes were left untouched. This source-backed candidate remains unmeasured visually.
