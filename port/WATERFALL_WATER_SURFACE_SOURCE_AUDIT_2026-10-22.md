# Waterfall-top water-surface/flow UV audit — 2026-10-22

**Scope and boundary.** Read-only comparison of authored Windows source/XML and the current Linux port. No code, tests, build, GPU/display, or existing reports were changed. Here “water surface” means the visible `waterfall_4.png` flowing sheets, not a separate simulated water mesh: `waterfall_top.xml` authors these as `ParallaxLayer`s. Prior `WATERFALL_TOP_VISUAL_GAP`, `WATERFALL_AUTOSCROLL_IMPLEMENTATION`, and `WATERFALL_AUTOSCROLL_GPU_REVIEW` already cover the eight-sheet V-scroll implementation and physical-GPU observations; the splash 49/50/51 reports cover distinct particle emitters. This audit isolates an untested **shader-path exception** to that scroll, not a new general autoscroll finding.

## Source contract

- `Game/XmlFiles/levels/waterfall_top.xml:261-307` authors water sheets **IDs 13/14** as `Media/waterfall_4.png` `ParallaxLayer`s at `kGroundBack`, each 810.662903 × 1621.325806, alpha 0.3, horizontal flip true, `effectpixelwobble` intensity **0.005** and speed modifier **10**, `autoscrolly=true`, speed **11**; neither follows camera Y nor has camera-Y UV parallax. The other six `waterfall_4.png` sheets (IDs 6, 7, 10, 15, 16, 31 at `:109-209,309-355,563-585`) use ordinary light-texture rendering. No independently authored water-surface object or dedicated water shader is evident in this level XML.
- Windows `Game/src/GameObjects/ParallaxLayer.cpp:114-155` advances the **vertex** V coordinate from its previous value by `speed * .001 * lastUpdateDelta / targetDelta` on every draw, including the non-bump pixel-wobble effect. `Game/src/GameObjects/Sprite.cpp:675-710` draws that same vertex buffer for `effectpixelwobble`. `Game/ShaderFiles/BasicLightTexture_PixelWobble.fx:55-66` then perturbs those interpolated UVs (V first, U second) and samples with wrap and `MIN_LINEAR_MAG_POINT_MIP_LINEAR` (`:13-19`). Thus wobble is **in addition to**, not a replacement for, directional V flow. `ParallaxLayer::Scale` also scales positive Y speed (`ParallaxLayer.cpp:225-232`), so the phase rate depends on the Windows backbuffer/game scale; at the port’s 720/1080 Y scale and 60 nominal updates, speed 11 corresponds to .44 V cycles before wobble.

## Current port gap

`port/game/LevelScene.cpp:363-369,677-685` parses and advances per-layer autoscroll for IDs 13/14, and `:926-945` forwards the accumulated value in `render::Sprite::uvShiftY` together with pixel-wobble state. But `port/linux/SDLRenderer.cpp:207-212` selects `DrawPixelWobble` **before** the wrapped-UV copy branch at `:230-244`. `DrawPixelWobble` at `:317-375` constructs corner UVs only from flips (0/1) and never reads `sprite.uvShiftY` or `uvShiftX`; its GLSL at `:609-623` perturbs and samples those unshifted UVs. Therefore the wobbling ID13/14 texture can animate locally while its authored downward phase is lost at the final renderer seam. This is a source/port path mismatch, not proof of a visible pixel defect in a matched Windows capture. The existing GPU review measured IDs 6/10/7 and an eight-sheet composite; it did not isolate phase progression of IDs 13/14, so its “eight-sheet” observation does not close this exception.

The SDL split-copy path's wrap fix and software tests cover non-wobble sheets, not this GL path. The port GL path already uses `GL_REPEAT`, linear minification and point magnification (`SDLRenderer.cpp:327-343`), appropriate to the source sampler; simply routing IDs 13/14 through SDL copies would discard the pixel-wobble shader and be incorrect. Keep their XML alpha, depth, flip, and geometry unchanged.

## One focused next implementation/test slice

In the renderer-owned GL pixel-wobble branch, compose the supplied `uvShiftY` into the shader's **input V before the first sine** (and X shift before that where relevant), with sign/orientation checked against the existing `1.0-uv.y` SDL-texture conversion. Do not add it only to the final sample coordinate: the source's second sine depends on already-perturbed V, and its first sine depends on the input U. Preserve `GL_REPEAT`, filtering, alpha, and the existing 10/5-second wobble clock.

Add one narrow deterministic test for IDs **13 and 14**: at fixed camera, assert nonzero/different successive V phases reach the pixel-wobble draw contract at 0/1/60 updates, while intensity=.005, speed=10, flip, alpha=.3 and destination remain constant; at zero wobble intensity, verify the shader's sampled row displacement matches the ordinary speed-11 sheet with the same UV orientation. Then, when the GPU lane is free, isolate IDs 13/14 in two fixed-camera active-play frames and check downward texture-feature motion *as well as* local wobble; compare a controlled Windows capture before declaring visual parity. No such test or capture was run in this audit.
