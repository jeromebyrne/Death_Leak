The existing NVE7 held-pull packet establishes that the current build renders the intro, **not** that shake causes any particular pixels: its adjacent ordinals have simultaneous zoom, follow, and animation changes, and the older packet has a different binary. ## Source seam and minimal private control

HUD is drawn after scene postprocess. `--simulate-only` skips this visual sample and is insufficient.
- Minimal private implementation: add a **test-only, explicit CLI switch** (e.g. `--capture-suppress-held-pull-shake`) accepted only with `--fresh-save-intro --play Game/XmlFiles/levels/forest_exploration_1.xml`, `--capture`, and without `--simulate-only`. Do not alter zoom, held clock, phase, input, landing `StartMedium`, camera follow, renderer, or postprocess. Default mode is on; switch is off. Emit the switch state and, in a private trace, frame index, held time, zoom, Start accepted/refused, remaining, follow axes, view/stored camera coordinates, world/player state and sign (if sampled). Keep this private toggle out of production delivery or remove it after proof. A compile-time source edit per arm is inferior because it produces two binaries and invites concurrent-tree drift.

The port caps delta at 1/60. No other process may alter source/assets during the pair. The only intended A/B causal difference is whether the intro shake request enters `CameraShake`; camera histories then legitimately diverge *because of that intervention*. “Same history” means matched initial state, deterministic non-camera state/input/time and RNG at the point of intervention, not falsely identical post-intervention camera coordinates. The current main's only `std::rand()` calls are shake sign draws; if a later change adds global-rand consumers, isolate or advance a matched private sign stream in both arms before interpreting post-start differences.

## Ordinals and pixel discriminator

`--frames N --capture ...` saves loop frame `N-1` before `EndFrame`. With immediate `I:220`, frame 0 (N=1) is the first held/accepted request, but `CameraShake::Update` returns the *pre-jitter* view: A/B world pixels should match at N=1, apart from unrelated wall-clock film or rendering nondeterminism. Frame 1 (N=2) is the first possible view divergence. This is a source-derived expectation, not a prior captured observation. The existing packet's pull log is frame 165 (N=166); inspect trace to confirm the same strict-threshold ordinal in both arms rather than assuming it if source changes.

Capture both arms at minimum `N=1,2,3,6,7,8,14,15,16,60,61,150,160,161,164,165,166,167,170,180,196`. N=1 is pre-visible control; N=2/3 reveal first-lag displacement, N=6–16 span expiry/restart windows without assuming an exact six-frame cadence, N=60/61 exercise grown accepted amplitude, N=150–165 are late held, N=166 threshold/zoom reset, N=167–170 possible natural tail, and N=180/196 post-pull/completion negative controls. Run separate identical-history processes per ordinal, or use an owner-approved multi-frame dumper without changing simulation order. Do **not** compare N=165 to N=166 as a pure shake test: zoom resets there. For release-tail proof, add a second common script with a short held interval then `.` release, matching A/B ordinals around release and ≥0.10 s afterward; do not infer release behavior from `I:220` alone.

Compare A/B at each *same* ordinal in lossless BMP, never adjacent on-only frames or old binaries. First require bitwise or tightly reproducible equality in world and HUD regions at N=1, equal player position/phase/held time/zoom/shader clock and capture dimensions throughout, and identical threshold/completion ordinals. Exclude animated foliage/orbs, particles, translucent film, frame edges, prompt, and HUD from world registration. Register multiple opaque static landmarks (e.g. left branch crop near `(20,270,150,95)` and bridge near `(450,515,550,90)`, adapting masks to avoid moving sprites). Use a subpixel translation fit or difference of edge centroids, with local crops rather than a single global MSE; world layers have parallax, so estimate each landmark separately. Hold same-ordinal zoom fixed between arms. The source predicts primarily horizontal A/B displacement, consistent signed direction across independent static landmarks after projection, **no systematic Y translation**, and identical fixed HUD red-bar bounding box `(43,46)–(90,51)` (observed in prior packet). Static-world difference should appear no earlier than N=2, track the view-X trace and follow damping, and disappear after shake/lag tail settles; the film overlay can prevent whole-frame equality even when world shake is gone. A null integer-pixel shift at N=2 is *not* a failure: first intensity is only `5*(1/60)/2.75 ≈ 0.0303` world units, passed through follow lag and rasterization. Check late held frames for enough subpixel/pixel evidence, and corroborate with trace. If world differences appear at N=1, Y-only drift, mismatched zoom/player/phase, or HUD bar displacement, reject causal attribution and investigate.

For scale, one accepted event adds at most its latched intensity (≤5 world units before threshold) to stored X per update, decaying over 0.10 s; forest-1 Y is not shaken. A hypothetical isolated 5-world-unit view shift projects to at most `5*(2/3)/0.75 ≈ 4.45` logical horizontal pixels at minimum held zoom, *before* follow damping, interpolation and layering. This is **not** a hard bound on cumulative A/B camera separation or the pixel-difference bounding box: successive jitter steps and camera follow accumulate, and a translated image changes many edge pixels. Use the logged view-X delta and known `2/(3*zoom)` projection to set per-ordinal expected translation/sign and a tolerance commensurate with subpixel rasterization; require repeated, coherent landmark agreement rather than a fabricated fixed pixel-count threshold. The expected HUD displacement is exactly zero in its opaque anchor geometry.
