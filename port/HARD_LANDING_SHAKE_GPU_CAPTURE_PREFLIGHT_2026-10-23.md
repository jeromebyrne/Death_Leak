## Expected positive and negative evidence

- **N=27 (airborne), N=28 (contact): negative control.** The event first occurs during update 28; the frame-28 view was snapshotted before stored-camera jitter. Thus no *new same-frame scene displacement attributable to shake* versus A is expected. Contact spray/player animation may be visible in both. If B diverges by a rigid shake-sized shift on 28, recheck frame indexing, build identity, and camera sampling.
- **N=29 onward, through the 0.30 s window: positive.** At least one static world landmark should have a nonzero X offset from same-ordinal A caused by the **previous** stored-camera perturbation; the sign may vary per run/frame. The camera's ordinary follow/clamp can attenuate or carry an offset, so compare a temporal series and report measured displacement rather than insisting each selected frame has nonzero delta. Forest-1 Y should have no injected jitter. Player, foreground/background world layers, combat world items, rain/weather world draws should shift coherently by their respective camera transforms; screen-space health/focus/HUD bands/film remain anchored. Frame 29 is the first primary positive target; 30/32/36 provide the temporal discriminator.
- **N=44/46/48: decay/expiry controls.** With fixed ~60 Hz timing, medium shake expires about 18 updates after start (around N=46). There is no new perturbation once expired, but a residual stored-camera difference may take follow lag to settle; **do not assert exact pixel equality at 46/48**. Show shrinking/ceased new jitter and eventual convergence under X follow; for a strict no-event reference use A and precontact B. A trigger should not start again merely because the player stays grounded.
- **Reject false positives:** HUD itself shifts; scene shift appears on contact frame but not after; only landing spray/animation pixels differ; Y jiggles despite follow-Y=false; a still image alone is declared parity; renderer is software/dummy; A/B binaries include unrelated source differences; contact did not occur or is an authored initial placement. A source-Windows visual parity claim needs a matched original executable/source identity, frame timing, view/stored camera trace, and active-gameplay image—none currently supplied.
