## New finding: combat is composited over **all** scene foreground

Windows sorts every `GameObject` by descending depth (`Game/src/GameObjects/GameObjectManager.cpp:58-64,1024`) and draws that single ordered list (`:302-329`). Its layers put NPCs at `kNpc=3500`, the player at 3000, player knives at `kPlayerProjectile=2500`, then `kGroundFront=1750`, far/middle/near foreground at 1500/1000/500 (`Game/src/GameObjects/GameObject.h:35-56`). `Player::FireWeapon` actually constructs the knife at `kPlayerProjectile` (`Game/src/GameObjects/Player.cpp:425-463`). Forest-1 authors multiple `kGroundFront`, `kFarForeground`, and `kMiddleForeground` objects (`Game/XmlFiles/levels/forest_exploration_1.xml`, e.g. lines 102,132,177,613). The foreground should therefore paint **over** a knife (or NPC) where their visible pixels overlap, with source alpha preserved.

The port correctly stable-sorts XML scene sprites by descending depth (`port/game/LevelScene.cpp:546`) and draws that entire list in `LevelScene::Draw` (`:820-904`), including ground-front and foreground layers. But active gameplay calls `scene.Draw` first and only then `combat.Draw` (`port/linux/main.cpp:1106-1115`). `Combat::Draw` draws every NPC and shot after the scene regardless of source depth (`port/game/Combat.cpp:488-520`). Consequently all port combat pixels, including the now correctly faded player knife, sit on top of even the nearest foreground. This is a definite ordering semantic mismatch in the exercised held-fire route, independent of camera/timing or texture sampling. The 9-second still confirms visible knives, but does not isolate an opaque knife/foreground pixel overlap or quantify the resulting image delta.

This is higher priority for the reviewed route than late weather, and more defensible than modifying source-authored foreground art or global brightness. It is **not** a claim that it explains the scene-wide lower darkness/right shaft; its visual footprint is limited to dynamic actors/projectiles at overlaps. No matched original Windows frame is available for a pixel-parity or acceptance claim.

## Correction boundary and proof gate

Do not apply a one-line reorder such as drawing all combat before the scene: that would place NPCs behind the player/background, and would lose the distinct 3500/2500 source layers. A correct change needs a unified or interleaved painter stream for XML scene sprites/sprays, NPCs, player knives and other shots, with source layer values and stable same-depth ordering; it must also account for port-only spawned orbs and health pickup currently drawn outside the scene's ordered loop. This crosses `LevelScene`, `Combat`, and the main render integration, so no **narrow** safe correction was justified in this bounded review. No production source, art/XML, test, build, new capture, commit, push, or shared status/checkpoint file was changed.

For the implementation lane: use an instrumented renderer or simple opaque test quads at distinct source depths to assert a `3500 → 3000 → 2500 → 1750 → 1500/1000/500` submission order, plus a same-camera foreground-over-knife compositing test. Then rebuild in the established Docker image, recapture 10 seconds on the exclusive physical GPU, inspect an actual knife/occluder overlap crop, and compare against a frame-matched Windows reference if available. The current clip establishes active gameplay and path exercise, not visual acceptance.
