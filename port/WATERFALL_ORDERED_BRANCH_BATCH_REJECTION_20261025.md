# Waterfall-top ordered branch-batch discriminator — rejected (2026-10-25)

## Scope and mechanism

At HEAD `0e87a68`, seven unrelated tracked paths were dirty, including unstaged raft-mip work in `port/linux/SDLRenderer.cpp`. The isolated source/build, trace and capture artifacts are ignored under `port/build/waterfall-ordered-batch-20261025/`; no production source or asset was edited. The prototype was built from clean HEAD source and executed on physical Xorg `:0`, SDL x11/OpenGL accelerated=1/software=0, nouveau NVE7, 1280×720.

The Waterfall-top 3000–1001 interval has player/dynamic objects at 3000, ground-front at 1750 and far foreground at 1500. Within ground-front, XML IDs 52/53/54 are a consecutive ordinary `branch_3`, `branch_3`, `branch_2` painter run. IDs 52/53/54 have effectlighttexture, no repeat, UV shift or rotation, and use the existing opt-in six-asset branch atlas page. The first two virtual IDs share a source rectangle; the third changes virtual texture and flips X. Native `DrawSprite` copies are minified and use linear sampling. The test-only `DEATHLEAK_BATCH_WATERFALL_BRANCHES=on` collected just these three in original source order and submitted one same-page linear geometry call, preserving other scene objects, sprays, private shaders, fog/water, render targets and HUD as boundaries. No broader scene reorder was attempted. The renderer's existing HUD geometry batch was not changed in production.

## Serial physical evidence

One isolated binary was used with `DEATHLEAK_ATLAS_SCENE_BRANCHES=on` in all arms; only the test batch switch changed off/on/off. Fixed-dt scripted `L:60` stayed in Waterfall-top and ended at `(366.985,2594.58)` in every arm. Serial apitrace counted actual `glDrawArrays` **8198 / 8197 / 8198** and `glBindTexture` **13353 / 13352 / 13353**: exactly one driver draw/bind saved in 60 frames. That is an actual GL boundary, but not a recurring per-frame batch benefit. The `L:180` route crossed into Waterfall-bottom; it yielded 14691 / 14690 / 14691 draws and 25930 / 25929 / 25930 binds, again just one saved call over the entire route. It cannot serve as sustained Waterfall-top performance evidence.

Native-clock `L:60` final BMP controls matched but candidate differed, consistent with the known process-uptime-driven full-screen film; that result cannot establish batch pixel parity. Rebuilding the *same isolated candidate source* with a test-only ordinal film clock and repeating serial physical `L:60` produced identical full 1280×720 BMP hashes in all three arms (`f7bd7657...8621f49`, zero changed RGB pixels). A test-only `L:180` untraced A/B/A also produced identical final BMP hashes (`5e783fe7...d13061d`) and matched bottom-scene gameplay endpoints. The deterministic film clock is not shipping. These endpoint checks do not prove every intermediate branch frame exact; rejection does not depend on an unproven full-route pixel claim.

Untraced `L:180` frame-latency samples (120 warmup, 60 sampled, largely after crossing into Waterfall-bottom) p50/p95/p99 ms: **16.9668/17.4258/45.7574 → 16.9556/17.2292/44.5934 → 17.0153/17.4929/44.3094**. They overlap and include the transition; no Waterfall-top latency gain or sustained-FPS result is supported. The traced 60-frame run has no post-warmup latency sample.

## Disposition and remaining assets

**Reject for production.** The real GL saving is one draw and one bind over either route, while the implementation adds scene-specific buffering and a special sampler path. No prototype source, artwork or test clock is staged or shipped. This is distinct from the earlier particle rejection (SDL already coalesced that run) and from the accepted HUD batch (seven real draws/binds saved per frame). It does not establish 60 FPS, broad batching, 8K/high-Hz support, VRAM/thermal stability or release acceptance.

Remaining unbatched classes: ordinary scene trees/blur, cave/ground props and shafts (atlas sampling/visibility and residency still need individual proof); repeating/UV-shift ground, waterfall sheets and fog; private pixel-wobble/foliage shader draws and render targets. Tree-blur and rock opt-in runtime mappings remain rejected on prior exact-pixel or unchanged-GL grounds. The next useful candidate needs *repeated per-frame* measured GL boundaries in an eligible ordered run, or a measured shader/pass cost reduction; another branch-only queue is not justified by this trace.
