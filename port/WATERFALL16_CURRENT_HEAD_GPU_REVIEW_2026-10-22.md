# Screenshot 16 versus current-HEAD waterfall-top: bounded physical-GPU review (2026-10-22)

## Verdict

A live, active `waterfall_top.xml` NVE7 frame can reproduce the **three defining static landmarks** of Steam screenshot 16 in approximately the same horizontal arrangement: the two separated inward-sloping raft tips and the upper-right ledge tip. In the closest captured sample (`leftjump-f82.png`), their approximate 1280-wide X positions are **~525, ~705, ~740**, versus **~540, ~700, ~730** read from the resized store still. These are manual visual estimates, not transformed-corner measurements or an exact fit. The broad pale fall, dark flanking shafts, and near-bottom foliage also appear in both. No source-proven missing/misplaced raft, ledge, or waterfall layer follows from this pair. Do not change XML/art or transform order on this evidence.

The conspicuous differences are **unmatched gameplay and output state**, not a bounded renderer defect: screenshot 16 shows an airborne player around (675, 220), HUD orb count **24**, partially depleted health, and multiple visible uncollected orbs; the Linux candidate has player below the viewport while falling, count **3**, full health, and fewer visible orbs after its scripted route. The store JPEG is letterboxed/processed (black bands around y=0–30 and y=645–719 in the 1280 resize), whereas the SDL BMP fills 1280×720. Fog density, waterfall flow/UV phase, ambient weather and JPEG softness are not controlled between the images; apparent fine texture/contrast or foliage-edge differences cannot be assigned to a code gap. This is **qualitative composition parity only**, not pixel or Windows-renderer parity.

## Interpretation and limit

The static raft/ledge group shifts together under scripted left motion, as expected from the authored X-follow camera; this resolves the older ~320-px displacement as a camera-framing seed rather than a proved object-placement error. The candidate's raft slopes, central separation, upper-right ledge and large curtain agree qualitatively after translation. The right-raft orb remains visible near its ledge; missing center/upper orbs in the candidate cannot establish missing render content with collection states different. The candidate is farther into a fall than the original airborne pose, so player visibility and foreground occlusion are not a like-for-like test. The original's viewport transform/camera, orb state, player position and animation, effect/weather clocks, and source backbuffer remain unknown. A lossless logged original-Windows frame is needed to turn any residual into an implementable gap.
