# Next still-unported active-play effect: material footstep particles (2026-10-22)

## Verdict and priority boundary

The clearest **recurrent, source-proven, still-unported focal visual** in forest-1 and waterfall-top is the three-particle material spray emitted at authored player footfall animation frames on dry solid lines. It follows the moving player during ordinary traversal, so it is a stronger next active-play candidate than a late weather transition or a one-off pickup effect. This ranking is by trigger frequency and focal placement, **not** by measured screen area or Windows/Linux pixel delta: each burst is small and short-lived. It cannot explain a broad fog, sepia, shaft, water-sheet, or foreground composition mismatch. No screenshot or GPU comparison was made here.

This is separate from already audited/implemented ID47 oak leaves, waterfall splash IDs 49–51, orb trail, ninja death spray, firing arm, player shadow, aim line, knife spawn fade, weather, and foliage phase. The larger landing/jump bursts in the same source are related but **not** included in the recommended first slice; their contact/timing behavior needs independent validation.

## Exact source-to-port contract

- Forest Player ID2 uses `XmlFiles/animation/player_anim_default.xml` (`Game/XmlFiles/levels/forest_exploration_1.xml:33-48`); waterfall-top also has a Player ID2 (`Game/XmlFiles/levels/waterfall_top.xml:33-48`). That animation marks Running frames **0 and 10** with `sfx_type="footstep"` (`Game/XmlFiles/animation/player_anim_default.xml:189,277`), as well as strafe and roll frames (`:367,377,445,515,987,1017`). The forest starting ground is `SolidLineStrip` ID3 with material `grassground` (`forest_exploration_1.xml:61-85`); waterfall-top has authored `grassground` solid lines, e.g. ID11 (`waterfall_top.xml:211-235`). `materials.xml:173-179` selects the sole active `grassground` particle texture `Media\\rock_particle.png` (the grass/wet alternatives are XML comments); softwood and caverock have their own particles (`:100-104,138-142`). These are existing assets and authored metadata, not proposed art edits.
- In Windows, `Character::UpdateAnimations` calls `DoAnimationEffectIfApplicable` on the run/strafe branch, then records the last frame (`Game/src/GameObjects/Character.cpp:1010-1062`). `DoAnimationEffectIfApplicable` requires a *new* animation frame bearing SFX and a current solid line/material; non-water material with a nonempty particle texture emits exactly **3** particles at `(playerX + directionX*12, CollisionBottom()+17)`, at character depth, with direction `(-.5,.5)` when facing right or `(.5,.5)` otherwise (`Character.cpp:1119-1176`). Its directed spray has spread .1, bounds 1200×720, speed 2–4, lifetime .3–.7 s, size 40–50, gravity .5, brightness .8–1, lifetime size scale 2.5, and fade-in/out .15/.7. The event is gated by the animation frame and solid-line material, not a free-running timer or every movement update. The same routine is also invoked for roll (`:899`); the first bounded slice can target Running while retaining the metadata for later strafe/roll coverage.
- Linux reads body frame filenames/rates but discards `sfx_type` at `port/game/LevelScene.cpp:381-402`; its Running sequence advances frames at `:935-973`. `port/game/LevelScene.h:160-168` stores only frame texture IDs and rates. Ground collision in `port/game/PlayerMotion.cpp:120-145` iterates segments but does not expose their material to this animation path; `port/game/LevelScene.cpp` has no load/draw reference to `rock_particle.png` or `softwoodparticle2.png` (targeted source search). Existing `ParticleSpray` instances in scene/combat are for authored level sprays and combat effects, not player footfall material emissions. Therefore merely seeing ordinary ground sprites or ID47 leaves in a port capture does not close this omission.

## Bounded implementation and discriminator

Preserve the authored per-frame `sfx_type` when loading the player body animation. On **transition into a flagged Running frame**, and only with a dry current solid-line contact whose material has a particle texture, emit one three-particle spray using the source's position, facing, character depth and parameters above. Resolve the current contact material from the actual grounded segment rather than guessing from the background sprite or hardcoding all surfaces to grass; a forest-1 ID3 and waterfall-top grass-line case can form the initial scope. Reuse the port's scene-owned particle renderer/update contract, including source brightness affecting alpha, with seeded randomness for deterministic checks. Do not alter XML, texture art, unrelated weather/scene layers, combat spray behavior, or movement physics just to make the spray visible. Handle strafe/roll and separate jump/landing bursts only after the Running path is verified.

A focused CPU discriminator: step the Running animation across frames 0 and 10 with a known grassground contact, assert exactly one three-particle emission per flagged **frame entry**, correct `rock_particle.png`, origin/facing/depth, and no emission on unflagged frames, airborne motion, water material, or repeated ticks of the same frame. Then, when the reserved GPU owner is free, isolate a same-camera player-foot crop at a logged flagged frame and 0.1–0.5 s afterward: small textured flecks should appear and decay at the character's feet in the corrected Linux path, absent in its baseline. A matched Windows comparison additionally needs the same animation frame, contact line, facing, camera and RNG/history; this source audit does not claim that match.

**Scope:** source/XML/port and prior audit notes only. No build, Docker, GPU/Xorg, gameplay run, source change, shared-report edit, or parity claim.
